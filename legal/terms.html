6. Blocking, notifications and wellness — how they work and their limits

The App is a focus tool. When you start a focus task, the App blocks the apps you have selected by displaying an overlay over them, until the task is marked complete or the maximum block-time you set has elapsed.

Important: Blocking on Android relies on system permissions you grant (Usage Access, Display over other apps, battery-optimization exemption, and optionally Notification Access). If any of these permissions are revoked, restricted by the operating system, or limited by your device manufacturer, the blocking feature will weaken or stop working.

You acknowledge and agree that:

  • Blocking is provided as a self-imposed, opt-in tool. It is not guaranteed to be unbypassable. A determined user with access to the device can disable the App, revoke permissions, force-stop the service, uninstall the App, or use other devices to access blocked apps.
  • Brief unblock windows may occur after a device reboot, during low-memory conditions, or when the operating system places the App into Doze or App Standby.
  • A focus task can only be finished once its minimum duration has elapsed. It ends automatically at the maximum duration you set, or after 10 hours if you did not set one. Blocking does not depend on an internet connection.
  • You are solely responsible for your choice of which apps to block and for any consequences of being unable to access those apps during a focus task (including but not limited to missed messages, calls, alerts, or time-sensitive tasks).

The App is not intended for use in any context where being unable to access a particular app, or missing a notification from it, could cause harm to life, health, safety, or property — for example, do not block apps you may need in an emergency, and do not suppress notifications you may need to receive.

6.1 Notification blocking

When you create a focus task, and if you grant Notification Access, you can have the App dismiss notifications while that session is running — either from the apps you selected, or from every app on your device. Both options are off unless you choose them.

Read this before choosing “All Apps”. That option dismisses notifications from every app except BrainClean for the whole session. That includes calls, messages, work and school alerts, delivery and banking alerts, and reminders you may be relying on. Dismissed notifications are removed from your notification shade and the App cannot bring them back. Do not use this option if you may need to be reachable, and do not rely on it in any situation where a missed alert could affect anyone’s health, safety, or property. Choosing it is your decision and you accept responsibility for anything you miss while it is on.

Notification blocking depends on Android continuing to deliver notifications to the App. Some notifications — for example certain system, emergency, or ongoing notifications — cannot be dismissed by an app at all, so you should not assume every notification will be suppressed.

6.2 Wellness features

The App also offers two optional features, both switched off unless you turn them on: Eye Blinker, which shows a periodic reminder to rest your eyes, and Doom Scroll Breaker, which interrupts an app with a short countdown once you have used it continuously for longer than a limit you set. Like blocking, these depend on the permissions you grant and on your device’s behaviour, and may not trigger at exactly the moment expected.

Not medical advice. Eye Blinker is a general-purpose reminder timer, not a medical device, diagnosis, or treatment, and it is not a substitute for professional advice about your eyes or your health. Do not rely on it as one. If you have concerns about eye strain or your vision, consult a qualified professional.
